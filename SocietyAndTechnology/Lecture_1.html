Part One

What is society?

A group of people living together, sharing culture, values, and rules — working together to meet needs and keep order.

Society is a structured community of individuals who share a common culture, social norms, values, institutions, and patterns of relationship.
8 traits

What makes up a society

Every society, big or small, shares these building blocks.

Social structure

How family, school, religion, government, and the economy are arranged to keep things running.

Family raises children · schools educate · government keeps order.

Culture

The shared way of life — language, food, dress, customs, and belief — passed down through generations.

Pohela Boishakh in Bangladesh · Cherry Blossom Festival in Japan.

Social norms

Unwritten rules that guide behaviour — what's okay, and what isn't.

Respect elders · follow traffic rules · stand in a queue.

Interaction & relationships

Society exists because people talk, work, and connect — building trust and cooperation.

Family · friendships · teacher–student · employer–employee.

Diversity & stratification

People differ by religion, language, and class — which enriches society but can also create inequality.

Upper, middle, and lower class groupings.

Collective identity

A shared feeling of belonging — to a nation, religion, culture, or language group.

Strong identity → more unity and cooperation.

Change & dynamics

Society never stays still — it shifts with technology, education, politics, and globalisation.

Letters → smartphones and social media.

Functionality

Every institution has a job — together they keep the whole system working.

Hospital heals · business produces · school teaches.
InstitutionFunction
FamilyRaises children and teaches values
SchoolProvides education
GovernmentMaintains law and order
HospitalProvides healthcare
BusinessProduces goods and services
Part Two

What is technology?

Applying scientific knowledge to build tools, machines, and systems that solve problems.

Technology is the collection of tools, systems, and processes created through human knowledge and innovation to solve problems, increase efficiency, and improve quality of life.
5 kinds

Types of technology

Information Technology

Computers and digital systems that process, store, and send information.

Computers · smartphones · internet · cloud · databases.

Mechanical Technology

Machines used in manufacturing and transport.

Car engines · factory machines · robots · elevators.

Biotechnology

Using living organisms or biology to make useful products.

Vaccines · genetic engineering · DNA testing.

Nanotechnology

Working with extremely tiny particles.

Nano medicine · nano sensors · nano coatings.

Communication Technology

Tools that let people exchange information across distances, instantly.

Mobile phones · email · video calls · social media.
Why it matters

What technology does for society

1 · Problem solving

Technology handles everyday and complex problems efficiently — GPS finds locations, medical equipment diagnoses disease, online banking saves time. Net effect: less time, less effort, more output.

2 · Economic impact

Technology drives development — but not without trade-offs.

Gains
  • New industries
  • Higher productivity
  • More jobs created
Costs
  • Automation replaces some workers
  • Traditional jobs disappear

3 · Social change

It reshapes daily life — online shopping, digital payments, online education, and social networking have transformed how people live, learn, and connect.

The two-way street

How they shape each other

Society builds technology to solve problems. Technology, in turn, quietly rewires how society communicates, learns, works, heals, behaves, and creates.

01 / Communication

Talking, instantly, everywhere

Apps like WhatsApp, Messenger, and Zoom changed how people stay in touch.

Community buildingOnline groups let people with shared interests connect — Facebook groups, Reddit, gaming communities — but can trap people in echo chambers, where only agreeable opinions get through, weakening critical thinking.
UpsideInstant reach · global connection · easy sharing · better collaboration
DownsidePrivacy risk · fake news · cyberbullying · mental strain
02 / Access to information

Knowledge, on demand

Google, YouTube, and online courses put learning within reach anytime, anywhere.

Digital divideNot everyone has internet, a computer, or a smartphone — this gap widens social and economic inequality.
UpsideFree resources · learn anywhere, anytime
DownsideMisinformation spreads fast, confuses and misleads — always check trusted sources
03 / Employment

New tools, new work

Automation uses machines, robots, and AI to do work once done by hand.

Work cultureRemote tools (Zoom, Teams) bring flexible hours and no commute — but also isolation and blurred work-life lines.
UpsideFaster, cheaper, more accurate production
DownsideJob displacement · new skills required · more machine dependence
04 / Health & wellbeing

Care, closer to home

Telemedicine brings consultation to remote areas without travel. Wearables track heart rate, sleep, steps, and oxygen levels.

Mental healthOveruse of screens can bring stress, anxiety, addiction, and poor sleep — balance matters.
UpsideFaster access to care · better self-tracking · useful for rural areas
DownsideScreen overuse linked to anxiety, depression, sleep disorders
05 / Ethics

Who's watching, who decides

Apps and platforms collect huge amounts of personal data, raising privacy risks. AI now makes decisions in hiring, lending, and driving.

AI concernsBias, lack of transparency, accountability, job loss, and fairness all need responsible handling.
Watch forData theft · identity theft · surveillance · biased AI decisions
06 / Environment

Powering up, cleaning up

Solar, wind, smart grids, and electric vehicles cut pollution and save resources.

E-wasteOld phones, computers, and batteries pollute soil and water if not recycled properly.
UpsideLess pollution · efficient energy · sustainable growth
DownsideElectronic waste · harmful chemicals · health risk
07 / Culture

Sharing the world, losing a bit of home

YouTube, Facebook, and Instagram expose people to cultures far from their own — while AI, digital art, and video editing open new creative doors.

Trade-offGlobal exchange can flatten local traditions into a single dominant culture — plus new questions over copyright and authenticity of AI-made work.
UpsideCross-cultural understanding · new creative tools · global audience
DownsideFading local traditions · cultural homogenisation · ownership disputes
In short

The takeaway

Society and technology are locked in a loop: people build tools to solve problems, and those tools reshape how people communicate, learn, work, heal, and create.

The benefits are real — but so are the costs: privacy loss, misinformation, job displacement, mental strain, and environmental damage. Used with care, technology can lift the quality of life for everyone; used carelessly, it can widen the very gaps it promised to close.
